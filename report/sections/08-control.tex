\subsection{The run: climb, decelerate, hold}
\label{sec:run}

An autonomous run has three stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:states}). In Climb, both motors run at full power. The robot has no way to measure distance, so Climb ends at a set time after the start. Pitch is the backstop on that time: if the pitch stays below \ang{18} for \qty{40}{ms} before the set time, Climb ends then instead. The robot ignores this test for the first \qty{0.3}{s} of the run. In Decelerate, the drive power ramps linearly from full to zero over a set time, and momentum carries the robot from the ridge onto the mesa. It then enters Hold.

In Hold, the robot sets its motor power to zero while it is level, and the driver code applies zero power as short brake. Short brake connects each motor's two terminals together. A motor turned by an outside force then acts as a generator driving current through its own windings, and that current makes a torque opposing the motion, $\tau_\mathrm{brake} = k_\mathrm{t}^2 \omega / R_\mathrm{w}$ for a motor turned at speed $\omega$.

Hold also corrects the robot's position from its pitch. If the nose rises more than \ang{6}, because the robot stopped short of the top or is being shoved back down our ramp, it drives forward at full power, 255 of 255. If the nose drops more than \ang{6}, because the robot is about to go over the far side, it backs up at 110 of 255. It keeps correcting until the pitch is back within \ang{3}, so that it does not switch rapidly between correcting and braking at the edge of the \ang{6} band.

\subsection{Tuning}
\label{sec:tuning}

We empirically found that a climb takes about \qty{3.3}{s}, so we set \qty{3150}{ms} of full-power climb and a \qty{150}{ms} deceleration ramp. We also built a trim tool for straight-line driving: each press shifts power \qty{16}{\percent} toward the lagging side, and the step halves every time the drift reverses. The larger problem turned out not to be the robot pulling sideways but the side guards rubbing on the treads (Section~\ref{sec:layout}).
