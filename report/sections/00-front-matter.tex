\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
We designed and built an autonomous tracked robot for the AME 101 Monarch of the Hill contest. Two robots start at the bottoms of opposite \ang{33} ramps, and after \qty{30}{s} the robot whose farthest point is closest to the center of the shared summit wins. Our strategy was to reach the summit first and never depend on a pushing match: a low wedge with forks at the front lets an arriving opponent drive up and over us, and running along a side wall replaces steering. The robot uses two \qty{25}{mm}, \qty{280}{\rpm} gearmotors on the stock sprockets with sandpaper on the tracks, a SparkFun TB6612FNG motor driver and a Particle Photon~2 controller. It recognizes the summit with a complementary filter that combines gyro and accelerometer readings, because the accelerometer alone misreads pitch whenever the robot speeds up or slows down. It climbs the \qty{1.52}{m} ramp in about \qty{3.3}{s} (timed by stopwatch) and weighs \qty{1.000}{kg} against the \qty{1}{kg} limit. We won the contest with a 5--1 record. Our one loss, in the final round, came from run-to-run variation in the timed advance past the crest: the robot stopped short, and the opponent's farthest point ended up closer to the center. Our main lesson is to triage hard: dropping complex ideas early left enough time to test the robot and its software.
