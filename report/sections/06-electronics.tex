\section{Electronics and power}
\label{sec:electronics}

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:electronics} shows the wiring. A Particle Photon~2~\cite{photon2} runs the robot. It replaced the kit's ESP32 board, which failed during bench wiring for reasons we could not determine.

The kit's motor shield drives its motors through a co-processor commanded over a serial link; we replaced it with a SparkFun TB6612FNG dual motor driver~\cite{sparkfun} we had on hand, controlled directly from the Photon's pins and with its motor supply wired straight to the battery.

An MPU6050~\cite{mpu6050} gyro and accelerometer on the I$^2$C bus does all the sensing, read by firmware written mostly with Claude Code~\cite{claude}. Power comes from the supplied 2S pack of 18650 cells. A judge starts the run with a single switch, which the Photon reads directly.

The Photon takes \qty{5}{V} on its VUSB pin and makes its own \qty{3.3}{V} for logic, so every sensor and signal line runs at \qty{3.3}{V}. The 7805 regulator supplies that \qty{5}{V} from the pack (Section~\ref{sec:current-bound}). We bench-tested the regulator with the capacitors we had, two \qty{100}{nF} in parallel on the input and one \qty{100}{nF} on the output, below the \qty{0.33}{\micro F} input capacitor the datasheet calls for~\cite{reg7805}, and measured \qty{5.05}{V} out.

\subsection{Resets from a loose cell}
\label{sec:resets}

During bench testing the Photon sometimes restarted. Stalling both motors never caused a restart, but jerking the robot by hand with the motors idle did, which ruled out a supply dip from motor current. A jolt could slide the battery holder into a wall of the box hard enough to compress a contact spring and break contact for an instant. Fastening the holder fixed it.
