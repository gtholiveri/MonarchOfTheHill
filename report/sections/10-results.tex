Our one loss, in the final round, came from pacing the climb by time. We reached the mesa first and ended up under the opponent, as designed, but the robot stopped short of where it usually did. The motors run at full power for a fixed \qty{3.15}{s} and then ramp down, and once on the mesa Hold drives forward only while the nose is still up, so where the robot stops varies from run to run with track slip, vibration and other random factors. That run it fell short, and because the opponent's footprint was much smaller than ours, its farthest point was closer to the mesa center, and we lost on that rule.
